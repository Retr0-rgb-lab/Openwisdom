## Nature and function of art

Objectivism defines art as a selective re-creation of reality according to an artist's metaphysical value-judgments. The artist selects a subject and presents it through a medium and style, concretizing an implicit view of what is important, possible, and worthy of contemplation.

Art serves a cognitive and psychological need: human beings hold very broad abstractions about existence, man, choice, success, suffering, and value, but cannot experience abstractions directly. Art gives a perceptual form to such judgments and can provide emotional fuel or confirmation of a sense of life.

Art is not:

- A substitute for philosophical proof.
- A transcript of ordinary reality without selection.
- Defined solely by political message or moral correctness.
- Guaranteed great because the artist intended an admirable theme.
- Rendered worthless merely because its philosophy is false.

## Components of analysis

### Subject

What entities, events, or human concerns are presented? Selection itself indicates importance, but do not infer a complete worldview from subject alone.

### Theme

What abstract meaning does the work integrate? State it as a proposition broad enough to explain the major elements but specific enough to be testable against the work.

### Plot or structure

In literature and drama, plot is a purposeful progression of events arising from conflicts of values and choices. In other media, analyze the analogous organization of elements, attention, tension, and resolution.

### Characterization or focal entities

Are characters intelligible agents with motives, values, choices, and consequences? Distinguish intentional idealization or stylization from inconsistency and cliché.

### Style

How does the artist select and shape details? Consider clarity, abstraction, composition, language, rhythm, color, form, performance, medium, and integration. Style expresses a psycho-epistemological approach but should not be diagnosed from one superficial feature.

### Technique

Evaluate command of the medium relative to the work's chosen goal. Technical polish does not guarantee artistic depth; roughness can be intentional but still must serve the work.

## Romanticism and sense of life

Romanticism emphasizes volition: purposeful agents choose values and actions with consequential outcomes. Naturalism often emphasizes forces outside individual control. Do not use these as simplistic quality rankings or assume every tragic ending is naturalistic.

A sense of life is a preconceptual emotional appraisal of existence and one's relation to it. Questions include:

- Is the universe presented as intelligible or unknowable?
- Are values possible and important or futile and fraudulent?
- Are human beings efficacious, helpless, corrupt by nature, heroic, comic, or mixed?
- Do choices matter?
- Is achievement admired, trivialized, punished, or impossible?

Infer a sense of life from integrated patterns across the work, not from one dark color, unhappy event, or villain's speech.

## Objective judgment and personal response

Separate at least four judgments:

1. Classification: Is it art in the relevant Objectivist sense?
2. Craft: How effectively are medium, structure, and technique controlled?
3. Integration: Do subject, theme, plot, characterization, and style cohere?
4. Philosophical content and personal response: What view is concretized, and does it resonate with the reviewer?

Personal enjoyment depends on legitimate individual context and values. That does not make every claim about technique or meaning equally valid. Conversely, an objectively integrated work need not become a personal favorite.

An artistically strong work can concretize false or malevolent ideas. A philosophically congenial work can be sentimental, didactic, incoherent, or inept.

## Esthetic workflow

1. Identify the work, medium, version, and relevant cultural context.
2. Describe before evaluating.
3. State the apparent subject and theme with evidence from the work.
4. Analyze selection, structure, style, technique, and internal integration.
5. Infer metaphysical value-judgments and sense of life cautiously.
6. Distinguish the artist, narrator, character, and audience; a character's line is not automatically the artist's doctrine.
7. Evaluate philosophical content separately from artistic achievement.
8. State personal resonance as personal while explaining the values involved.
9. Compare with the work's own purpose and medium, not an alien standard.
10. Give an integrated judgment and the strongest alternative reading.

## Safeguards

- Do not treat art as propaganda whose merit equals ideological agreement.
- Do not use fictional causality as empirical evidence outside the work.
- Do not attribute every character statement to the artist.
- Do not infer evasion or a corrupt sense of life from taste alone.
- Do not force representational boundaries or medium disputes into certainty when Objectivist scholars disagree.
- Do not call a work Romantic merely because it is optimistic, heroic, futuristic, or contains action.
- Do not call a work Naturalist merely because it is tragic, realistic, or depicts social constraints.
- Do not confuse moral admiration for a subject with excellence of execution.
- Do not confuse disgust at a theme with a demonstration that the theme is present.
