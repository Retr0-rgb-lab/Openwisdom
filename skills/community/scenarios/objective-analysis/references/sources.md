## Governing source rule

Use sources for two different jobs:

1. Philosophical fidelity: What did Rand or Objectivism hold, and how is the system integrated?
2. Factual truth: What is actually the case in the concrete issue?

The highest-fidelity Objectivist source can still be wrong or outdated about a concrete fact. The best factual source does not by itself establish an Objectivist evaluation. Join the two only through an explicit derivation.

Synthesize compatible philosophy, scholarship, and contemporary application into one answer. The source roles below guide research and attribution; they are not mandatory output compartments.

## Source roles and working priorities

### Foundation: Ayn Rand

- Material written by Ayn Rand.
- Material explicitly endorsed by Rand as a presentation of her philosophy.

Start with the [Ayn Rand Institute's works catalog](https://aynrand.org/novels/) and the actual work in a lawful edition. ARI's FAQ says that Objectivism is presented in material Rand wrote or endorsed and that each person must judge whether later positions are consistent with it: [ARI FAQ](https://ari.aynrand.org/faq/).

### Systematic bridge: Leonard Peikoff and curated primary passages

- Leonard Peikoff, Objectivism: The Philosophy of Ayn Rand, based on the course Rand endorsed as an accurate presentation of the full theoretical structure: [official overview](https://aynrand.org/novels/objectivism-the-philosophy-of-ayn-rand/).
- The Ayn Rand Lexicon, edited by Harry Binswanger, for locating primary passages by concept: [Lexicon index](https://aynrandlexicon.com/lexicon/).
- ARI Campus recordings and texts when the provenance and speaker are clear: [ARI Campus](https://courses.aynrand.org/).

Use the Lexicon as an index and contextual aid. Verify decisive quotations in the underlying work when possible.

### Development: later Objectivist scholarship

Useful authors include Leonard Peikoff, Harry Binswanger, Allan Gotthelf, Gregory Salmieri, Tara Smith, Onkar Ghate, Robert Mayhew, Darryl Wright, Yaron Brook, and other scholars with relevant expertise. Their work can deepen, extend, and apply the foundation to new questions; judge each contribution by its integration, evidence, and explanatory power.

Use strong later work as part of the integrated Objectivist analysis. Do not repeatedly downgrade it with labels. Preserve exact attribution when the user asks, when thinkers disagree, or when the reasoning depends on a genuinely new or contested development.

High-value works include:

- Harry Binswanger, How We Know.
- Allan Gotthelf, On Ayn Rand.
- Allan Gotthelf and Gregory Salmieri, editors, A Companion to Ayn Rand.
- Tara Smith, Viable Values and Ayn Rand's Normative Ethics.
- Leonard Peikoff, The Art of Thinking; Understanding Objectivism; Objectivism Through Induction; Introduction to Logic; Objective Communication.

Judge arguments and evidence rather than counting credentials.

Useful locators:

- [Leonard Peikoff courses and biography](https://courses.aynrand.org/people/leonard-peikoff/)
- [Objectivism Through Induction](https://courses.aynrand.org/campus-courses/objectivism-through-induction/)
- [A Companion to Ayn Rand](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/9781118324950)
- [Ayn Rand Society](https://www.aynrandsociety.org/about)
- [Ayn Rand Society Philosophical Studies](https://upittpress.org/series/ayn-rand-society-philosophical-studies/)
- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Ayn Rand](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/)
- [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Ayn Rand](https://iep.utm.edu/rand/)

Use competent critical scholarship for adversarial testing and historical context. Do not dismiss it because its author rejects Objectivism, and do not let an external summary override primary text when attributing Rand's position.

### Contemporary applications and source discovery

Search broadly across relevant institutes, scholarly organizations, lecture and course archives, journals, magazines, author sites, podcasts or transcripts, and competent criticism. Choose sources for their relevance to the concrete question rather than because they appear on a fixed preferred list.

Use contemporary commentary to locate arguments, applications, facts, and primary-source leads. For each material item:

- Identify the individual author, date, expertise, and type of piece.
- Separate philosophical derivation from factual reporting and rhetorical framing.
- Trace consequential factual claims to primary or independent evidence when possible.
- Compare contrary evidence and other serious Objectivist or critical analyses.

Multi-author outlets can vary substantially by contributor and article. Evaluate the item, not the publication's general orientation. Do not mention a site merely because it was consulted. Name or cite it only when it directly supports a material claim, quotation, distinctive argument, or attribution; clarifies a material disagreement; or the user requests sources.

### Independent field evidence

For current events, science, law, medicine, economics, history, or technical subjects, prefer:

- Primary documents, datasets, statutes, cases, official records, and direct transcripts.
- Peer-reviewed research and systematic reviews suited to the question.
- Domain experts working within their competence.
- High-quality reporting that links to evidence and distinguishes news from analysis.

Use multiple independent sources when the claim is disputed or consequential.

## Ayn Rand's published works map

### Fiction

- We the Living: individual life under dictatorship; strongly autobiographical context but still fiction.
- Anthem: individual identity, collectivism, rediscovery, and the conceptual importance of "I."
- The Fountainhead: independence, integrity, creation, second-handedness, and projection of an ideal.
- Atlas Shrugged: the most extensive fictional integration of reason, production, rational egoism, trade, sanction, rights, and capitalism.
- Night of January 16th: drama, conflicting character types, and audience judgment; not a systematic philosophical source.
- The Early Ayn Rand: early stories, plays, and developmental material; use publication and revision context.
- Three Plays: dramatic craft and selected themes across Night of January 16th, Ideal, and Think Twice.
- Ideal: response to professed ideals and the cost of integrity; distinguish novel and play forms.

### Nonfiction

- For the New Intellectual: broad philosophical and cultural framing.
- The Objectivist Newsletter: essays and applications from 1962–1965; verify individual authorship.
- The Virtue of Selfishness: the Objectivist ethics, rational self-interest, moral concepts, rights, and government.
- Capitalism: The Unknown Ideal: moral and political case for capitalism plus economic and historical applications.
- The Objectivist: essays, reviews, and applications from 1966–1971; verify author and date.
- Introduction to Objectivist Epistemology: concepts, measurement-omission, definitions, objectivity, and the problem of universals: [official overview](https://aynrand.org/novels/introduction-to-objectivist-epistemology/).
- The Romantic Manifesto: nature and function of art, Romanticism, literature, and sense of life.
- The New Left / Return of the Primitive: culture, education, environmentalism, and anti-industrial ideas; treat concrete claims in historical context.
- The Ayn Rand Letter: essays and current applications from 1971–1976; distinguish period facts from principles.
- Philosophy: Who Needs It: philosophical method, axioms, metaphysical versus man-made, philosophical detection, faith and force, and cultural applications.
- The Ayn Rand Lexicon: topic-organized excerpts edited by Harry Binswanger; use as locator, then recover original context.
- The Voice of Reason: later essays and applications.
- The Ayn Rand Column: short newspaper writing and public applications.
- Ayn Rand's Marginalia: private comments on other writers; valuable but not equivalent to developed published argument.
- Letters of Ayn Rand: biography, development, and private application; preserve recipient and date.
- Journals of Ayn Rand: drafts and intellectual development; do not treat every note as settled doctrine.
- Russian Writings on Hollywood: early criticism and historical context.
- The Ayn Rand Reader: curated selections useful for orientation, with underlying works controlling context.
- Return of the Primitive: expanded edition of The New Left containing later material whose authorship must remain clear.
- The Art of Fiction and The Art of Nonfiction: writing and esthetic craft.
- Ayn Rand Answers: useful Q&A with variable spontaneity and context; do not elevate an off-the-cuff answer over a developed published argument.
- Objectively Speaking: interviews whose interviewer, date, editing, and transcript status should be preserved.

The official works catalog lists the principal fiction and nonfiction: [Novels & Works](https://aynrand.org/novels/).

- ARI's [Introduction to Objectivism](https://aynrand.org/ideas/philosophy/) summarizes reality, reason, rational self-interest, and capitalism while linking to deeper material.
- ARI's [Overview](https://aynrand.org/ideas/overview/) gives a concise orientation.

## Logic and cognitive-bias sources

### Accessible starting catalogs

- [Your Logical Fallacy Is](https://yourlogicalfallacyis.com/) supplies 24 accessible fallacy examples. Its FAQ explicitly presents the list as introductory rather than exhaustive.
- [Your Bias Is](https://yourbias.is/) supplies 24 accessible cognitive-bias examples and likewise directs readers to broader catalogs.

The catalogs in this skill include all 24 items from each site, clearly tagged, then expand them. Definitions are independently paraphrased; the posters are not copied.

### Argumentation and fallacy theory

- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Fallacies](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/fallacies/)
- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Informal Logic](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-informal/)
- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Argument and Argumentation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/argument/)

These sources emphasize context, argument schemes, dialogue, and evidence. A familiar inference pattern can be reasonable in one context and fallacious when critical conditions fail.

### Judgment and decision science

Use original or high-quality review literature when a bias finding is important. Starting points include:

- Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman, "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases," Science 185 (1974), DOI 10.1126/science.185.4157.1124.
- Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky, "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk," Econometrica 47 (1979), DOI 10.2307/1914185.
- Baruch Fischhoff, "Hindsight ≠ Foresight," Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance 1 (1975), DOI 10.1037/0096-1523.1.3.288.
- Charles G. Lord, Lee Ross, and Mark R. Lepper, "Biased Assimilation and Attitude Polarization," Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 37 (1979), DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.37.11.2098.
- David Dunning and Justin Kruger, "Unskilled and Unaware of It," Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 77 (1999), DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.77.6.1121.
- Noba's open psychology materials: [Noba Project](https://nobaproject.com/).

Bias research contains overlapping constructs, boundary conditions, failed replications, and statistical artifacts. Verify current reviews before making consequential or diagnostic claims.

Useful review and methods sources:

- Raymond Nickerson, "Confirmation Bias: A Ubiquitous Phenomenon in Many Guises," DOI [10.1037/1089-2680.2.2.175](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.2.2.175).
- Ziva Kunda, "The Case for Motivated Reasoning," DOI [10.1037/0033-2909.108.3.480](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.108.3.480).
- [Noba: Judgment and Decision Making](https://nobaproject.com/modules/judgment-and-decision-making).
- [Noba: The Replication Crisis in Psychology](https://nobaproject.com/modules/the-replication-crisis-in-psychology).
- [ASA statement on p-values](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf).
- Hernán and Robins, [Causal Inference: What If](https://miguelhernan.org/whatifbook).

## Internal source awareness and material disputes

If useful during research, use these tags internally:

- RAND_CORE: Rand's completed published foundational position.
- RAND_ENDORSED: A presentation Rand explicitly endorsed.
- PEIKOFF_EXPOSITION: Peikoff explaining the system, with work and date.
- SCHOLARLY_DEVELOPMENT: A later philosophical integration or extension.
- CONTEMPORARY_APPLICATION: Application to a concrete issue using additional factual premises.
- CRITICAL_INTERPRETATION: A competent external interpretation or objection.
- ANALYST_INFERENCE: The current agent's own derivation.

Do not display these tags or produce separate source-lineage sections unless the user asks or attribution changes the answer. Surface genuine disputes only when they remain material after integrating the strongest evidence and reasoning. Examples that may require this treatment include:

- Whether the validation of ethics is best framed around the choice to live, survival, flourishing, happiness, or an integrated account of these.
- The precise scope and derivation of rights and the non-initiation-of-force principle.
- Emergency contexts and conflicts among values.
- Intellectual property, pollution, children, abortion, immigration, government financing, war, and novel technologies.
- Boundaries of art and medium-specific disputes, including photography and architecture.
- How much doctrinal weight to place on spontaneous Q&A, private letters, journals, or later edited material.
- Polemical historical assessments of other philosophers.
- Material written by Nathaniel Branden or other associates: verify authorship, date, Rand's endorsement if any, and later revisions.

Rand's work supplies the grounded foundation. Later thinkers can validly explain, extend, and apply it to subjects she never addressed. The goal is the strongest integrated Objectivist conclusion, with source distinctions surfaced only when needed for accuracy.

## Research protocol

1. Define the exact philosophical and factual questions separately.
2. Locate the primary Rand or endorsed source for the principle.
3. Integrate later scholarship and applications while checking their bridge premises and evidence.
4. Locate current primary or expert evidence for the concrete facts.
5. Check dates, definitions, samples, methods, incentives, and source independence.
6. Seek the strongest contrary evidence and a plausible alternative interpretation.
7. Derive the conclusion explicitly rather than attaching a quotation.
8. Cite the source nearest the claim it supports.

When sources disagree:

- Do not average them.
- Identify whether the disagreement concerns facts, concepts, inference, value standard, or application.
- Prefer the source with the stronger evidential method for factual issues.
- Prefer Rand's developed or endorsed presentation for the content of Objectivism.
- State residual uncertainty and the evidence that could resolve it.

## Copyright and quotation

Paraphrase the philosophical framework in original language. Use only brief verified quotations when the exact wording matters. Do not embed books, paywalled articles, or substantial excerpts in the skill. If the user supplies lawful copies, search them for the task while respecting quotation limits.

Never fabricate a quote, page number, edition, date, endorsement, or doctrinal consensus. If exact language cannot be verified, label the statement as a paraphrase.
