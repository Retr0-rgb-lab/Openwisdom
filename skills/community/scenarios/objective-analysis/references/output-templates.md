## Quick audit

### Bottom line

One direct sentence stating whether the claim is well-grounded, qualified, mixed, unsupported, contradicted, or indeterminate.

### Decisive issue

Identify the most important fact, hidden premise, concept, or inference. Explain it without a taxonomy dump.

### Objectivist lens

Name the principle and show the derivation in two or three steps.

### Better formulation

Offer a corrected statement when the user is writing or arguing.

## Full audit

### Bottom line

Give the verdict and confidence basis.

### Strongest formulation

State the charitable, precise version of the target. List alternative readings only when they matter.

## Self-audit and rewrite

Lead with the revised text, unless the user asks for analysis first.

### Revised version

Preserve the user's intended point, tone, and audience. Narrow only what the evidence cannot support.

### Material corrections

| Original issue | Why it weakens the statement | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overbroad quantifier | Evidence supports some, not all | Narrow scope |

### Residual uncertainty

State any premise the revision still assumes.

Do not bury a strong rewrite beneath a lecture. If the intended conclusion is untenable, say so and offer the nearest defensible claim rather than laundering it into persuasive prose.

## Decision or policy audit

### Decision

State the recommended option or why no option is yet justified.

### Governing facts and constraints

List the identities, causal constraints, time horizon, actors, rights, and uncertainties.

### Alternatives

| Option | Expected life-serving values | Costs and risks | Rights or force | Reversibility | Key uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

### Objectivist derivation

Move explicitly from facts and human requirements to ethics and then politics. Distinguish:

- Personally prudent or moral.
- Immoral but legally protected.
- A rights violation properly prohibited.
- A legitimate retaliatory government act.
- A disputed application requiring more facts or legal philosophy.

### Failure conditions

State what would make the recommendation wrong.

Separate technical execution, internal integration, thematic meaning, and the reviewer's optional response.

### Objectivist context

Explain whether the work presents human beings as efficacious or helpless, existence as intelligible or unknowable, values as possible or doomed, and choice as consequential or illusory. Do not infer artistic quality from ideological agreement alone.

## Verdict language

Prefer these formulations:

- Well-grounded in the stated context.
- Sound in core, but overstates scope or certainty.
- Contains a valid concern supported by an invalid argument.
- Reaches a plausible conclusion from insufficient evidence.
- Uses an objective principle but misapplies it to the facts.
- Factually supported but ethically or politically underived.
- Objectivist in conclusion, not in method.
- Currently arbitrary: no evidential route has been supplied.
- Contradicted by the available evidence.
- Indeterminate between specified alternatives.

Avoid:

- "This is objective" without stating the method and context.
- Numerical objectivity scores that imply nonexistent calibration.
- Lists of fallacy names without reconstructing the inference.
- Claims about a person's subconscious motive based on one sentence.
- "Both sides are biased" as a substitute for weighing evidence.
