## Purpose

Use this reference to derive an analysis from Objectivism's base rather than matching isolated slogans. It is a compact operational synthesis, not a substitute for reading Ayn Rand or Leonard Peikoff.

## Foundation and development

Treat Objectivism as a grounded philosophy that can be explained, developed, and applied to new knowledge and new circumstances. Rand supplies the foundation; Peikoff systematizes and extends the integration; later Objectivist thinkers and publications can add valuable analysis and contemporary application.

The analytical goal is integration, not doctrinal bookkeeping. When later work is compatible with the foundation and well supported by evidence, use it directly in the unified analysis rather than downgrading it to a separate appendix or opposing "view."

For internal accuracy, retain enough source awareness to know whether an idea comes from Rand, Peikoff, later scholarship, contemporary commentary, or the current analysis. Normally keep that awareness out of the response. Surface it only when:

- The user asks what Rand or a particular thinker personally held.
- Two serious sources materially disagree.
- A contemporary conclusion depends on a disputed bridge premise.
- Exact attribution prevents misquotation or false authority.
- The history or development of the idea is itself relevant.

Source awareness protects accuracy; it is not a ritual hierarchy and never replaces proof.

## Philosophical hierarchy

Objectivism treats philosophy as a system:

| Branch | Fundamental question | Dependency | Core Objectivist orientation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Metaphysics | What exists, and what is its nature? | Base | Objective reality; existence, identity, causality; primacy of existence |
| Epistemology | How can a human being know it? | Metaphysics | Sense perception, reason, concepts, logic, contextual objectivity |
| Ethics | What should an individual value and do? | Metaphysics and epistemology | Human life as the standard; rational self-interest; virtues |
| Politics | What social conditions does human life require? | Ethics | Individual rights; ban on initiated force; objective government; capitalism |
| Esthetics | What is art and why does it matter? | All preceding branches | Selective re-creation of reality according to metaphysical value-judgments |

Logic is the method of noncontradictory identification within epistemology, not a disconnected list of verbal prohibitions.

Never derive a concrete conclusion by skipping intermediate facts. "Existence exists" does not by itself settle a tax rule, military operation, medical treatment, business decision, or artistic ranking. The derivation requires the nature of the entities, relevant causal evidence, human requirements, context, and field-specific knowledge.

## Axioms and principal corollaries

### Existence

Something exists. Existence is not a property added to a prior nothing; it is the widest fact presupposed by awareness and assertion. A purported denial must exist as an act concerning something and therefore relies on what it denies.

Operational checks:

- Does the claim treat wishes, labels, votes, commands, or narratives as if they create the underlying facts?
- Does it posit an alternative "outside" existence without a coherent referent?
- Does it confuse not knowing a fact with the fact's nonexistence?

### Consciousness

Consciousness is awareness of existence. Awareness has identity and requires content; it is not a creator of its object merely because it processes that object in a human form.

Operational checks:

- Is a feeling, perception, belief, model, or social consensus being confused with its object?
- Is the existence of consciousness used to deny access to existence, thereby using awareness to deny awareness?
- Is introspective evidence about a person's mental state improperly projected onto external reality?

### Identity

To exist is to be something specific. A thing is itself; its attributes and capacities follow from what it is. Contradictions cannot exist in the same respect at the same time.

Operational checks:

- Are the same entity, time, respect, and meaning held constant across an alleged contradiction?
- Does the proposal demand an action contrary to the entity's causal capacities?
- Is a change being treated as a violation of identity rather than an action of an entity with a specific nature?

### Causality

Causality is identity applied to action: entities act in accordance with their natures under the relevant conditions. It is not a relation among disembodied events or a mystical command imposed on reality.

Operational checks:

- Identify the acting entities, their capacities, conditions, and mechanism.
- Distinguish metaphysical causality from the epistemic evidence required to infer a particular cause.
- Do not infer a concrete cause merely from the axiom of causality.

### Primacy of existence

Existence is independent of consciousness; consciousness depends on and is directed toward existence. Human choices can rearrange existing entities according to their natures but cannot make contradictions real.

Contrast:

- Primacy of existence: identify facts, then choose action suited to them.
- Primacy of consciousness: treat wish, decree, faith, feeling, group agreement, or language as capable of overriding identity.

Do not use this contrast as a rhetorical club. Demonstrate the exact premise that grants consciousness fact-creating power.

## Objectivity

Objectivity is neither passive mirroring nor emotionless neutrality. It is volitional adherence to reality by a method appropriate to human cognition. The object supplies the content; the knowing subject performs the required processing.

Use the intrinsic-subjective-objective trichotomy:

- Intrinsic: Treats truth, value, or meaning as a feature grasped without a knower's method, context, or relation to a valuer.
- Subjective: Treats consciousness, preference, convention, or will as the creator of truth or value.
- Objective: Grounds the result in facts while accounting for the nature, method, and context of the human consciousness or valuer.

Examples:

- A measurement is objective when the unit, instrument, procedure, and relevant tolerances connect the result to the object.
- A value is objective when it identifies a factual relationship between something and the requirements of a living human being in context.
- An esthetic judgment can have objective components without implying that every personal response is identical.

## Metaphysical distinctions

### Metaphysically given and man-made

The metaphysically given consists of facts not created by human choice: natural entities, their identities, and events outside volitional control. The man-made consists of arrangements, institutions, artifacts, and consequences created through choices operating within natural constraints.

Audit:

- Do not morally condemn the metaphysically given.
- Do morally and causally evaluate human choices and institutions.
- Do not treat a man-made convention as unchangeable nature.
- Do not treat natural constraints as oppression or policy failures.

### Entity, action, attribute, and relationship

Keep the bearer of action visible. Reified abstractions such as "society decided," "the market wanted," or "history demanded" may abbreviate individual actions and relationships, but they must not erase the acting entities.

### Possibility

A live possibility must be compatible with known facts and grounded in some evidential path. Mere non-refutation does not make a scenario cognitively possible. Distinguish:

- Logical consistency in a thin description.
- Physical or causal possibility.
- Evidential possibility in the current context.
- Probability relative to evidence.

### Volition

Objectivism locates primary free will in the choice to focus one's consciousness. Do not infer a person's evasion, dishonesty, or culpability merely from error. Moral judgment also requires evidence about knowledge, alternatives, choices, and context.

## Branch audit

For any claim, ask in order:

1. What are the relevant existents, identities, causal capacities, and chosen arrangements?
2. What evidence and cognitive method connect the claim to those facts?
3. What human values, time horizon, alternatives, and virtues are implicated?
4. What use of force, fraud, rights, or governmental action follows, if any?
5. What abstract view of life is concretized if the object is art?

An answer can be consistent with Objectivism at one level and unsupported at another. For example, a policy may invoke individual rights while relying on false factual premises or an equivocal concept of force.

## Fiction and philosophical evidence

Rand's fiction is philosophically important because it dramatizes integrations of character, values, social systems, and consequences:

- We the Living: the individual and life under totalitarianism.
- Anthem: the meaning of the individual ego against collectivism.
- The Fountainhead: independence, integrity, creation, second-handedness, and an idealized individualist.
- Atlas Shrugged: the role of reason and production, rational egoism, trade, sanction, rights, and capitalism in a fully integrated plot.

Use fictional scenes and speeches to clarify meaning, illustrate a principle, or reveal a sense of life. Do not treat plot outcomes as empirical evidence that the same causal pattern holds in reality. When Rand's characters state philosophy, locate the corresponding nonfiction treatment or identify the use as literary presentation.

## Common failure modes

- Slogan matching: Calling a claim Objectivist because it praises selfishness, business, strength, or capitalism.
- Political inversion: Starting with a desired policy and retrofitting metaphysics or ethics.
- Rationalism: Deducing detailed facts from broad principles without observation or field knowledge.
- Intrinsicism: Treating a rule or value as context-free and self-executing.
- Subjectivism: Treating preference or group agreement as sufficient.
- Moralism: Judging character without evidence about choices, knowledge, and context.
- Doctrinal bookkeeping: Interrupting an integrated answer with unnecessary source-status labels.
- Source flattening: Treating all commentary as equally strong instead of judging its evidence and derivation.
- Fictional proof: Using a novel's outcome as a real-world causal demonstration.
- Authority substitution: Quoting Rand where evidence and argument are required.
