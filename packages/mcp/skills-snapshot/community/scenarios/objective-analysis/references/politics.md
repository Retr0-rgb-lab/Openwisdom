## Derivation from ethics

Politics asks which principles govern social organization. The Objectivist chain is:

1. A human being lives by using reason and acting on judgment.
2. In society, other people can offer immense values through knowledge, production, friendship, and trade.
3. Physical force compels action against judgment, paralyzes or redirects the victim's mind, and makes long-range life dependent on an aggressor's permission.
4. Social coexistence therefore requires a principle protecting each individual's freedom of action.
5. Individual rights identify that moral condition.
6. Retaliatory force must be placed under objective law to protect rights without private feuds or arbitrary coercion.
7. A rights-protecting government and laissez-faire capitalism follow at the broad political-economic level.

This chain requires facts about human nature, the effects of force, social interaction, institutions, and law. It does not settle every boundary or implementation detail automatically.

## Individual rights

Rights are moral principles defining and sanctioning an individual's freedom of action in a social context. The right to life is fundamental; liberty, property, speech, contract, association, and pursuit of happiness identify aspects or conditions of acting for one's life.

Rights:

- Belong to individuals, not collectives as organisms separate from their members.
- Protect freedom to act, not guaranteed outcomes.
- Impose a negative obligation on others not to initiate force or fraud.
- Do not create an unchosen claim to another person's labor, goods, affection, agreement, platform, or risk-bearing.
- Apply equally by relevant principle rather than by equalized result.

The legal right to do something does not imply that doing it is rational, moral, admirable, or immune from voluntary consequences and criticism.

## Force, fraud, and retaliation

### Initiated physical force

Identify:

- Actor and target.
- Concrete action or credible threat.
- Control imposed on body, property, movement, contract, or action.
- Whether the target consented and whether consent was informed and voluntary.
- Whether the action initiates coercion or responds to a prior rights violation.

Disapproval, persuasion, refusal, boycott, criticism, competition, offense, social exclusion, and a hard bargain are not physical force merely because they exert pressure or impose consequences. A monopoly created by voluntary success is not the same as a coercive monopoly backed by government.

### Fraud

Fraud uses material deception to obtain value or consent that would not otherwise be given, thereby subverting voluntary action. Not every falsehood is legally actionable fraud. Analyze representation, materiality, knowledge or recklessness, reliance, causation, harm, contract, and the governing law.

### Retaliatory force

Retaliation must be evidence-based, proportionate to the rights-protecting purpose, governed by objective procedure, and directed at the initiator or an actual threat. Vengeance, collective punishment, pretext, and arbitrary preventive force are not legitimized by calling them retaliation.

## Government and objective law

Objectivism holds that government is needed to place retaliatory force under objective control. Its proper functions are:

- Police to protect against domestic force and fraud.
- Courts to adjudicate disputes and apply objective law.
- Armed forces to protect against foreign aggression.

Objective law should be knowable, general in relevant application, based on demonstrable rights violations, constrained by evidence and procedure, and reviewable. Government is not exempt from the ban on initiated force.

Implementation questions such as financing, institutional design, jurisdiction, remedies, burdens of proof, emergencies, and transition from a mixed system require legal and empirical analysis. Integrate strong later scholarship and current evidence rather than forcing these conclusions into a separate doctrinal category.

## Capitalism

Laissez-faire capitalism is the social system based on recognition of individual rights, including property rights, with a separation of state and economics. It is not:

- Cronyism, subsidies, bailouts, licensing cartels, or regulatory favoritism.
- Any existing mixed economy merely because private firms operate in it.
- A guarantee that every business decision is rational or moral.
- An absence of law, courts, contract enforcement, or retaliation against fraud and force.
- Anarchism.

The Objectivist defense is primarily moral: capitalism protects the individual's freedom to think, produce, own, contract, associate, and trade. Economic productivity and coordination are vital consequences and evidence, not the sole moral foundation.

## Policy workflow

### 1. Define the proposal

State the exact rule, actor, jurisdiction, mechanism, funding, enforcement, timeline, exceptions, and intended result. Avoid evaluating a slogan such as "regulation," "freedom," "public good," or "capitalism."

### 2. Verify the problem

What harm exists? Who causes it? What rights, contracts, external effects, information failures, or ordinary business risks are present? Use primary data and law.

### 3. Identify rights holders and actions

Analyze individuals, not undifferentiated groups. Specify whose body, property, contract, speech, or liberty is affected and how.

### 4. Locate force

Trace every compulsory step, threat, tax, mandate, prohibition, seizure, licensing barrier, penalty, or retaliatory mechanism. Also identify private force, fraud, breach, nuisance, or aggression the policy may address.

### 5. Separate moral, legal, and prudential judgments

An act may be:

- Rational and legally protected.
- Irrational or immoral yet legally protected.
- A civil wrong requiring remedy.
- A criminal or hostile rights violation.
- A permissible government retaliation.
- A policy error that violates rights despite good intentions.

### 6. Compare alternatives

Include no action, voluntary action, private contract, tort or criminal enforcement, institutional reform, transitional approaches, and the proposed coercive measure. Compare rights first, then practical consequences within rights-respecting options.

### 7. Analyze consequences and incentives

Rights are not a license to ignore causality. Examine enforcement feasibility, knowledge problems, incentives, capture, evasion, unintended effects, concentration of power, transition costs, and reversibility.

### 8. Derive and qualify

State:

- Governing Objectivist principle.
- Case-specific factual premises.
- Legal or policy bridge.
- Conclusion.
- Confidence and unresolved application questions.

## Distinctions that prevent category errors

| Do not confuse | With |
| --- | --- |
| Immoral | Illegal or rights-violating |
| Offensive or false speech | Physical force or fraud |
| Private refusal | State prohibition |
| Economic bargaining power | Government coercion |
| Unequal result | Rights violation |
| Need | Enforceable claim on another |
| Competition loss | Sacrifice or expropriation |
| Voluntary charity | Altruistic duty |
| Public interest | A defined set of individual rights and values |
| National interest | Government's rights-protecting function |
| Existing business practice | Capitalism as a political system |
| Retaliation | Vengeance or preemption without evidence |
| Legal permission | Moral endorsement |

## Contested applications

Do not manufacture a source-by-source debate, but do not conceal a disagreement that would materially change the conclusion. Issues that may require extra factual or legal development include:

- Intellectual property boundaries.
- Pollution, nuisance, risk, and diffuse harms.
- Government financing and transition.
- Children, guardianship, and age of consent.
- Abortion and the boundary of individual rights.
- Immigration, citizenship, and security.
- War, preemption, proportionality, and civilian harm.
- Emergency powers and contagious disease.
- Antitrust and coercive versus market power.
- Defamation, privacy, digital fraud, and platform governance.
- Artificial intelligence, autonomous systems, and novel agency questions.

For these:

1. Identify the grounded Objectivist principles.
2. Integrate the strongest later reasoning and current evidence.
3. Map the factual and legal bridge premises.
4. Present competing derivations only when they remain materially live.
5. Conclude as strongly as the full evidence permits.
